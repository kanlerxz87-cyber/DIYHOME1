How many tiers does your cabinet actually need?

A short measurement routine that stops you buying eight tiers for a cabinet that fits four, using the ORDORA 8-tier rack as the worked example.

No hands-on testing· No search-volume or popularity claims· Product claims quoted from the listing

Most pan organisers are sold by tier count, because tier count is the number you can put in a title. Your cabinet does not care about tier count. It cares about internal height, the footprint of the pans you actually own, and whether you can reach the back without dismantling the stack.

This is the measurement routine, with the 8-tier rack linked from this site as the worked example.

Step 1. Count footprints, not pans

Take every pan out. Group them by diameter. A rack holds pans edge-on, so what limits capacity is the diameter of the largest pan in each slot and the thickness of the stack, not the number of pans in the cupboard.

A useful shorthand: three 28 cm pans stacked flat take the height of all three; the same three edge-on take the height of one plus the thickness of two rims. That is the saving you are buying.

Step 2. Measure internal height, twice

Measure from the cabinet shelf to the underside of the cabinet above or the closed door's inner face, taking the smaller number. Then subtract a hand's width for lifting a pan out. A rack that fills the cabinet exactly is a rack you will fight every evening.

Step 3. Check the stated dimensions against your number

The rack linked from this site states 16.6 inches in its title, and states that it fits cookware up to 11 inches. If your internal clearance is below the stated height, the rack is not the problem to solve; a shallower rack or a drawer divider is.

Step 4. Think about weight before you think about tiers

Adjustable wire racks are built to hold cookware at an angle, and their weak point is a heavy pan resting on a thin wire with nothing underneath it. The listing for this rack describes it for "Lightweight Cookware Up to 11"", which is the seller telling you where they think the limit is.

If your heaviest piece is a cast iron skillet or a stockpot, treat a wire rack as storage for the light pieces and keep the heavy one on the base of the cabinet. That is a decision you can make from the listing's own wording, without guessing at a load rating.

Step 5. Decide how you reach into the cabinet

A rack that loads from the front, with the cabinet door open in front of you, works in a narrow cabinet. A rack that needs access from both sides does not. If the cabinet sits beside an appliance, you have one usable side, so the tiers should run front to back rather than across.

Step 6. Assembly and reversibility

The listing title states "Tool-Free Assembly" and "3 DIY Modes", and describes the rack as non-slip and suited to cabinets, shelves and countertops. Tool-free and reversible matter in a rental: you can try it in one cabinet, decide it belongs in another, and move it without a drill.

What the title does not tell you is the footprint when assembled, or the height in each of the three modes. Those are the two numbers to look for in the listing photographs and product details before you commit.

The worked example, and its limits

Put together, the claims read directly from the official ORDORA listing are: 8 tiers, adjustable, 16.61 inch height across 3 assembly footprints (dual-vertical 21.31″W × 9.17″D × 16.61″H, single-vertical 11.75″W × 9.17″D × 16.61″H, horizontal 16.61″W × 9.17″D × 11.75″H), tool-free assembly, a seller-stated 10 lb per-tier limit, and stated suitability for lightweight cookware up to 11 inches in the title (bullet #4 says up to 12 inches, flagged on the product specification table). Those are seller-stated specifications read from the official listing, not independently load-tested by this site.

How many tiers do you need?

The answer that survives contact with a real kitchen is usually one tier fewer than the largest option. Fill the rack with the pans you use weekly, leave the occasional piece on the cabinet floor, and keep the clearance above the rack for your hand. If you are between two sizes, the smaller rack in a tidier cabinet beats the taller rack you cannot load.

What this guide does not claim

No claim that this rack is the best option, that it holds a stated weight, or that it will fit a cabinet it has not been measured against. No hands-on testing. No ratings or review counts, because Amazon's terms restrict that without API access.
